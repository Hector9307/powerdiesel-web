The D13 Is a Maintenance Engine

More than most engines its size, the D13 tells you how it has been treated. Overhead adjustment is not optional on these — the valve train wants its scheduled attention and gets unhappy without it. The EGR circuit, including the cooler and the venturi side, is where a lot of coolant and power complaints originate. The DEF pump and dosing hardware generate more derates than anything mechanical. And on the newer turbo-compound trucks there is extra hardware in the exhaust stream that has to be diagnosed rather than assumed. We work all of it on-site.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is the best Volvo D13 mechanic in Miami?

Power Diesel works D13 engines on-site across Miami-Dade and Broward, 24/7, including overhead adjustment, EGR work and DEF dosing faults. Call (786) 395-7263.

Does a D13 really need valve adjustments?

Yes, on the interval in your service manual. It is one of the few maintenance items on this engine where skipping it reliably costs more later, and we do it at your yard.

My D13 is losing coolant. Where do you look?

The EGR cooler is a common source on these engines. We pressure test the system and confirm the path before anything is opened, since the alternatives are far more expensive.

Is the D13 in a VNR the same as in a VNL?

The engine is the same family, but rating, calibration and duty cycle differ, and a regional VNR sees very different aftertreatment behaviour from a long-haul VNL. We diagnose against how the truck is actually used.

Can you handle D13 turbo-compound trucks?

Yes. The turbo-compound hardware adds components to the exhaust stream that need diagnosing rather than assuming, and we work them on-site.
