Medium Duty Is Its Own Discipline

An M2 106 delivering around Doral does something a highway tractor never does: dozens of stops a day, constant restarts, a body on the back that was fitted by somebody else, and a PTO or lift gate that the chassis was not born with. That duty cycle is what clogs aftertreatment on short runs, wears brakes fast, and creates the electrical headaches where the upfitter's wiring meets the Freightliner SAM modules. Diagnosing an M2 means understanding the whole package — chassis, engine and whatever is bolted to the back of it.

Why does my M2 keep needing regens?

Short-trip, stop-and-go delivery work never lets the aftertreatment reach temperature, so soot builds faster than it burns off. We look at regen history and the underlying EGR and dosing condition rather than just forcing another regen.

Can you work on the lift gate and body wiring too?

Yes. Upfit electrical is one of the most common M2 complaints we get, particularly where the body builder's wiring meets the Freightliner modules.

Is an M2 different from a Cascadia to work on?

Very. Different duty cycle, different brake systems on some configurations, different engines, and a body on the back that changes everything. That is why it has its own page.

Do you handle whole delivery fleets?

Yes. We run scheduled preventive maintenance for M2 delivery fleets across Miami-Dade and Broward, planned around your routes rather than against them.
